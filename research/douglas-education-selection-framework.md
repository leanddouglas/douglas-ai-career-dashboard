# Douglas: provisional education-to-employment decision framework

## Decision and scope

Choose exactly five **program candidates for a decision shortlist**, not five credentials to complete. Select one foundational credential from that shortlist, then add only short specializations justified by demonstrated job-skill gaps. If the broader research defines the five as a mixed list, label foundation versus add-on and do not directly compare them as substitutes.

This is a decision model, not verified program advice. No named institution, admission route, tuition, funding entitlement, employer preference, or employment timeline has been established here. All thresholds below are proposed decision rules. Douglas's budget, work timetable, province-specific attendance constraints, and target compensation remain to be confirmed.

Target outcome: team-based human-centered AI/software implementation work, grounded in Douglas's purpose: “I build intelligent systems that empower people and make everyday work genuinely easier.” Search adjacent role titles without treating titles as equivalent: AI application developer, AI solutions developer, AI implementation specialist, automation developer, and solutions engineer. Inspect duties: some solutions-engineer jobs are primarily sales, and some AI-engineer jobs require advanced modeling rather than application engineering. Do not quietly substitute help desk work for the stated goal.

## 1. Evidence packet before scoring

For each candidate record official program URL, exact credential and awarding institution, campus/delivery mode, intake, retrieval date, and source excerpts for:

- Admission with Douglas's actual education history: mature-applicant route, required assessments, English and math prerequisites, equivalencies, upgrading, and application deadlines. Citizenship alone does not establish eligibility. Obtain written admissions confirmation where published rules are ambiguous.
- Real part-time sequence and maximum completion period; mandatory live classes, exams, placements, labs, group meetings, course availability, and weekly workload including independent study.
- Full credential cost for the applicable domestic category: tuition, mandatory fees, materials, equipment, software/cloud/API spending, travel, prerequisites, financing, and any lost income. Distinguish quoted amounts from estimates.
- Official credential status, transfer/laddering arrangements, curriculum, assessed projects, collaboration, work-integrated learning, and whether portfolio work may be shown publicly.
- Funding sources: applicable provincial/federal program rules, institution aid, and employer support; identify part-time eligibility, enrollment thresholds, deadlines, reimbursements, and repayable versus nonrepayable money.

Separately collect a proposed minimum sample of 15 deduplicated first-party employer postings relevant to BC or Canada-remote work, emphasizing entry/junior and realistic transition roles. Preserve posting text/date because vacancies expire. Record duties, experience, required versus preferred education, degree-or-equivalent wording, technical skills, collaboration/accessibility/user-research expectations, location eligibility, and compensation where published. Report the sample's actual size and limitations; it is not a representative labor-market estimate. Use this matrix to define the curriculum target, not a program's marketing language.

## 2. Hard gates before weighted ranking

Use PASS, FAIL, or UNRESOLVED; never convert an unknown into a favorable assumption.

1. **Admission path:** direct entry or a documented, feasible alternative/upgrading route. Include its cost and time. A high-school-completion assumption is not evidence.
2. **Income preservation and schedule:** compatible with employment and a maximum of 10 study hours/week, including prerequisites, assignments, group work, commute, and exams. Plan for 8 committed hours plus 2 contingency hours. A compulsory unpaid placement or daytime requirement fails unless a specific acceptable arrangement is confirmed.
3. **Affordability:** gross cost and payment timing fit Douglas's agreed cash-flow ceiling, without relying on unapproved grants or unwanted borrowing.
4. **Relevant, verifiable award:** awarding body, credential type, and educational content are clear; the program teaches usable capabilities for the chosen job family.

UNRESOLVED candidates remain in an investigation queue. They cannot be presented as enrollment-ready. If fewer than five pass, broaden the search; never pad the shortlist with knowingly infeasible choices. Report the shortfall if evidence cannot establish five.

## 3. Weighted model: 100 points

Rate each dimension 0–4; 1 and 3 are intermediate anchors. Score = sum(weight × rating / 4). Gate failure overrides any total. These weights are proposed value judgments, not empirical estimates.

| Dimension | Weight | 0 anchor | 2 anchor | 4 anchor |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Admission feasibility | 14 | Confirmed unavailable route | Documented achievable upgrading/assessment required | Written or explicit published fit for actual background, with manageable conditions |
| Schedule fit | 16 | Exceeds 10 hours or conflicts with work | Fits at 9–10 hours with constrained sequencing | Verified flexible sequence fits 8 planned hours, including required attendance |
| Total cost | 12 | Exceeds agreed gross budget/cash flow | Affordable but consumes most agreed ceiling | Full gross costs comfortably fit agreed ceiling without lost income |
| Credential signal in Canada | 10 | Award/status opaque or unsupported relevance | Verifiable award with some relevance in posting evidence | Credential level/type consistently fits target postings and has documented progression value |
| Curriculum/job match | 18 | Predominantly unrelated content | Substantial relevant content but important gaps | Assessed coverage of most weighted target-role capabilities with no critical gap |
| Portfolio value | 10 | Attendance or quizzes only | Assessed projects with limited ownership or realism | Publishable, user-grounded, reviewed software with testing, deployment, documentation, and teamwork |
| Funding | 4 | No verified usable support | Verified eligibility, award not secured or small support | Confirmed nonrepayable/employer support materially improves payment burden without harmful conditions |
| Completion risk | 8 | Major unresolved barriers or inflexible progression | Manageable dependencies with support and restart options | Favorable sequence, support, pacing, leave/re-entry and credit retention; individual fit demonstrated |
| Time-to-employability | 8 | Useful demonstrated capability occurs too late for agreed horizon | Job-relevant milestones emerge midway | Early assessed milestones support credible applications while the credential continues |

Guardrails:

- Total cost scores gross affordability; funding scores confirmed assistance. Separately disclose net scenarios. This prevents counting the same grant twice. Loans are financing, not a tuition discount. Unapproved aid is zero in the base-case cash-flow plan.
- Schedule scores weekly logistics; completion risk scores prerequisites, sequencing, support, deadlines, and continuity. Do not reward the same flexibility twice.
- Credential signal is neither institutional prestige nor a job guarantee. Verify award type; “certificate” can mean materially different things. Institutional public status alone does not prove hiring value, and private status alone does not prove low value.
- Time-to-employability means time to evidence-backed application readiness, not promised time to a job. Include admission wait, upgrading, study load and hiring gaps; do not use advertised full-time duration for a part-time learner.
- Treat foundational software, data, privacy and team skills as critical curriculum capabilities. A strong AI branding score cannot compensate for their absence.

### Uncertainty, ranking and exactly five

Mark each input VERIFIED, ESTIMATED, or UNKNOWN with source/date. For unsettled ratings, show a defensible interval rather than a guessed midpoint; wholly unknown dimensions span 0–4. Calculate total lower and upper bounds. Do not imply a robust ordering where intervals overlap.

Start with a broad candidate pool. Remove gate failures and clearly dominated candidates; shortlist exactly five eligible, distinct programs using conservative scores and evidence quality. Do not force a university, bootcamp or vendor category into the five for variety. Each selected program must offer a real advantage and a stated trade-off. Show at least the strongest rejected alternatives and their exclusion reasons to expose cherry-picking.

Test ranking sensitivity by shifting five weight points at a time from curriculum fit to schedule, cost, and credential signal, and then in reverse, preserving the 100-point total. Also test a no-funding case and an 8-hour capacity case. If the winner changes, report the choice as preference-sensitive rather than objectively best. Only freeze the final five after each has passed the gates and critical facts are verified; otherwise label the list provisional.

## 4. Assessment that recognizes real skill without crediting AI output as mastery

Run a diagnostic before enrollment. Use familiar project contexts—OneBooks, Servus training systems, textile education apps or local automation—but use a new, unseen requirement. Confirm artifact ownership and protect workplace/customer data. Use synthetic records.

Suggested initial assessment: one normal study week, split into short sessions, with a hard 8-hour ceiling. Assess six dimensions: problem framing/user needs; coding/debugging; data/API integration; AI evaluation and reliability; security/privacy/accessibility; collaboration and explanation. This is diagnostic, not an admissions decision or an IQ/speed test.

### Three evidence passes

1. **Independent baseline:** no generative assistance, ordinary reference docs allowed. Explain an existing system and its data flow; implement a small unfamiliar change; debug a seeded failure; write tests; explain privacy and permissions. Record where help was needed. Do not grade memorized syntax or typing speed as engineering judgment.
2. **AI-assisted delivery:** use normal tools with an assistance log. Build a small human-centered workflow from an ambiguous brief, such as classifying synthetic service requests with a human review queue and an audit trail. Compare AI and deterministic alternatives, validate user need, define acceptance criteria, add error handling, tests and deployment instructions, and disclose cost/latency limits.
3. **Independent transfer and defense:** with AI off, explain selected code paths and design decisions; diagnose a newly injected failure or changed API response; change a requirement; identify insecure or incorrect AI suggestions; demonstrate a test that fails before the fix and passes afterward. A reviewer chooses the change, rather than Douglas rehearsing it.

Prefer concise observed tasks and a follow-up over making the entire assessment an unpaid production build. Allow breaks and accessible formats; don't infer inability from anxiety, literacy friction or a single timed performance.

### Common 0–4 rubric, reported per dimension

- **0:** cannot perform or explain the task, even with prompts.
- **1:** completes with step-by-step assistance; cannot reliably validate the result.
- **2:** independently performs familiar work using references; needs help adapting or diagnosing failures.
- **3:** independently adapts to new requirements, tests failure paths, and explains trade-offs.
- **4:** generalizes the skill, reviews others' work, and improves reliability/usability with evidence.

Keep independent proficiency and AI-assisted delivery scores separate. Project polish, code volume, brand-name tools, and confident explanations do not establish mastery. An AI-assisted score cannot erase a failed transfer test. Conversely, needing syntax references does not invalidate competence.

Placement policy: a dimension at level 3 across two different observed tasks supports skipping repetitive beginner instruction in that dimension. Level 2 supports a short targeted bridge; levels 0–1 justify foundational instruction. Do not average away a weak security, testing or ownership result. Formal credit exemption requires the institution's own prior-learning or challenge process; this rubric grants no institutional credit.

Reassess gaps after a targeted learning block. Diagnose mathematics in context—data types, units, percentages, basic statistics/probability, vectors if the selected application requires them. Add prerequisite mathematics only where verified admissions rules or the target work require it. Do not require traditional mathematics as a blanket condition for starting practical AI/software work.

## 5. One coherent credential spine, then targeted AI additions

Choose one accessible, part-time foundational credential with assessed programming, APIs, databases, Git, tests, security/privacy, software design and teamwork. Prefer documented laddering/credit transfer where it adds affordable future options, but verify it rather than assuming recognition. The award's exact level depends on verified admission, workload, budget and job evidence—not on a predetermined preference for a degree or bootcamp.

Then add at most two initial short specializations, sequentially and within the same weekly time budget:

1. **AI application engineering:** structured outputs/tool use, retrieval when justified, evaluation datasets, failure handling, security threats, latency/cost and deployment/monitoring. Take only after sufficient software/data foundations.
2. **Human-centered AI delivery:** discovery interviews, workflow analysis, accessible interaction design, human oversight, adoption, documentation and measurable outcomes. Substitute a narrowly targeted cloud credential only if posting evidence and the assessment justify that gap.

These are content targets, not claims about currently available programs. Do not enroll in two full workloads concurrently. Favor modules embedded in the foundation, recognized electives, or a single gap-filling short course over duplicative credentials.

Use a common portfolio thread: an existing project becomes a documented user problem, a small dependable application, an AI enhancement compared against a non-AI baseline, a tested human-review process, and a measured user trial. Obtain permission before using employer material. Add a real collaborative contribution with issue tracking, review and handoff; several solo AI-built apps do not prove readiness for team work.

Start application practice when the role-specific skills and project evidence are defensible; completion of every add-on is not a prerequisite. Use portfolio reviews and actual application feedback to decide whether more education is warranted. Do not promise a hiring date.

## 6. Adversarial review questions

- Does “mature admission” actually waive the missing requirement, or merely allow an application?
- Is “online” really asynchronous and compatible with exams, group meetings and placements?
- Does the stated workload include assignments, unfamiliar prerequisites and debugging?
- Is “AI” assessed application engineering or primarily tool demonstrations?
- Are job placement claims independently auditable, relevant to working part-time Canadian students, and explicit about denominator, withdrawals and job type?
- Does the credential satisfy actual posting requirements, or is the plan leaning on an exceptional-success anecdote?
- Can Douglas independently repair and defend the portfolio, and show collaboration rather than only prompt-driven output?
- Would one targeted module, documented prior-learning credit, or workplace project solve the gap more cheaply than another certificate?
- Does this path preserve income and energy at peak workload, not just in an average marketing week?
